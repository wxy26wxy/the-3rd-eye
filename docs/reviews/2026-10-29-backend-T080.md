### Important

1. **A combined selection cannot be trimmed, and the documented workaround discards the combination.**
   - Where: `web/lib/pipeline/artworks.ts:217-221`, `web/lib/api/artwork-schemas.ts:13`, `docs/api.md:116`.
   - What: negative points only shape the single SAM object; they never subtract from `candidateIds` masks. Candidate ids plus negative-only points are rejected (`400`) unless a box is added. The contract tells the client to drop `candidateIds` and send a box plus points instead.
   - Why: take a creator who combined two pieces (A + B) and wants to remove a shadow that came with A. The client has to throw away both stored masks and hope one SAM object prompted by a box around A ∪ B reproduces both pieces. SAM returns one object per id, and a box around two separated pieces tends to yield one of them or a blob that includes the gap. So the "combine, then remove a shadow" flow the brief asked for has no reliable path, and every attempt is a paid call. The brief's "unioned with candidateIds" also implied the labels apply to the combined result.
   - Fix: apply negatives to the candidate parts too. Before the union, drop from each candidate mask the connected component that contains a negative point. For a shadow attached to the object, also run SAM with that negative point as a positive and subtract the resulting mask from the parts. Allow candidateIds plus negative-only points. Add a test that combines k1 + k2, sends a negative point on k2, and checks that k2's region is gone.

2. **Re-cut on an artwork with products is not failure-safe, and the print check goes stale.**
   - Where: `web/lib/pipeline/artworks.ts:73-76`, `web/lib/pipeline/products.ts:47`.
   - What: `recut` commits the new `cleanedUrl` first (`finishArtwork` → `updateArtwork`). It then rebuilds each live product in turn, each with a paid Muse Image compose: about 16 s, with a 90 s timeout plus one retry.
   - Why, in four parts:
     - Budget: SAM, describe and N sequential composes share one 270 s deadline. Two slow composes give a 504.
     - Partial state: if product 2 fails, the shop already shows the new cut (`shopSelect` reads `artworks.cleanedUrl` live). Product 1 has new print files, while product 2 keeps the old print PNG, SVG and mockup. `runPipeline` writes `lastError` onto an artwork whose cut is fine.
     - No recovery path except repeating the whole cleanup, which repeats the SAM call and every compose. There is no "rebuild only".
     - Stale check: `listing.printCheck` is not recomputed. A creator can box a much smaller region on a published sticker, and the product stays published with a print file that would now fail `checkResolution`.
   - Fix: wrap each product's rebuild in its own try/catch and return or record which products failed, for example via `details.failedProductIds`. Run the composes with `Promise.allSettled` instead of sequentially. Recompute resolution and contrast in `rebuildProductFiles` and write them to `listing.printCheck`. If the check fails on a published product, move it to draft or block it. Fix the "allow up to 120 s" claim in `docs/api.md:119`: nothing bounds the time.

3. **The 0.5 % island rule silently drops legitimate small pieces of one candidate, and they cannot be recovered.**
   - Where: `web/lib/image/mask.ts:269,284`.
   - What: inside one part, components under 0.5 % of the largest blob are removed. At the 2048 px working scale, a drawing's main blob of about 500 k px gives a cut-off of about 2,500 px, roughly a 50×50 px piece.
   - Why: dots on an "i", sparkles, small stars and short separate strokes in lettering are typically in that range. They are not separate SAM candidates, so "combine" cannot bring them back. A box or point re-cut goes through the same `dropIslands`. Drawings are the primary flow, and this is a regression from the previous behaviour, which kept every SAM pixel. The brief asked for "drop islands under 0.5 %", but its intent was removing specks.
   - Fix: use an absolute floor at working scale, for example under 0.02 % of the frame or under 64 px, or combine the relative rule with a cap. Add a fixture: a disc plus a 30×30 dot well apart in one mask, and assert the dot survives.

4. **The second phrase is not checked against drawing photos, and the frame-coverage rule does not remove a sheet of paper.**
   - Where: `web/lib/ai/meta/sam.ts:31,199`.
   - What: `"handmade object"` was checked live only on two Muse-generated photos of objects. Nobody ran a photo of a drawing on paper lying on a table, which is the main flow. The 95 % frame rule removes only frame-filling results.
   - Why: a sheet covering 60–85 % of the frame can plausibly match "handmade object". It would be kept, because its IoU with the drawing inside it is low. When "drawing" finds nothing, that sheet becomes the only candidate and is auto-selected (`createFromImage`, `candidates.length === 1`), so the whole paper becomes the sticker with no picker shown.
   - Fix: run the opt-in live check on two or three real drawing-on-paper photos and record the result in docs/architecture.md. Also drop a candidate that contains another candidate and covers more than about 40 % of the frame ("container" rule), or skip auto-select when the chosen candidate covers more than about 50 % of the frame.

### Minor

1. **Off-by-one at the right and bottom edges of a box.**
   - Where: `web/lib/ai/meta/sam.ts:236`.
   - What: `clampX` and `clampY` cap at `sentW-1`/`sentH-1` and floor. A half-open box that reaches the image edge (`x1 = w`) is sent with `right = w-1`, and `buildObjectPrompt` then emits `x2 = w-2`, so the last column and row are excluded.
   - Fix: clamp the right and bottom to `[left+1, sentW]` and use `Math.ceil(v*sx)`.

2. **Docs say out-of-range values are clamped, but negative coordinates are rejected.**
   - Where: `docs/api.md:111` vs `web/lib/api/artwork-schemas.ts:4`.
   - What: the docs say "values outside the image are clamped", but `coord.min(0)` returns 400 for negatives. Only values past `w`/`h` are clamped.
   - Fix: change the doc, or clamp negatives.

3. **The SAM-failure fallback returns an arbitrary segment.**
   - Where: `web/lib/ai/meta/sam.ts:257`.
   - What: when SAM fails and no fallback segment contains a positive point and there is no box, `pickByHint` returns `allowed[0]`, which is an unrelated threshold blob (on photos, often the table). The cleanup then unions it with the creator's candidates.
   - Fix: return null when positive points were given and none hit a segment, so the request gives 422.

4. **A tiny selection gives a 500 instead of a 422.**
   - Where: `web/lib/image/sticker.ts:79`.
   - What: with a very small mask, the blur plus resize leaves no alpha above 127, so `removeBackground` throws a plain `Error("Mask is empty")`. That becomes a 500, and `runPipeline` writes `lastError` onto the artwork.
   - Fix: check `alphaToMask(alpha)` in `finishArtwork` and throw `ApiError(422, "no_drawing_found")`.

5. **The whole frame is upscaled when only the cut needs it.**
   - Where: `web/lib/pipeline/artworks.ts:53-55`.
   - What: `cutUpscale` upscales the entire original, often 4032×3024 → 8064×6048, about 49 MP. That size then flows through `finishMask`, the RGB raw buffer, `joinChannel` and PNG encode, two full-frame JS passes over the alpha, and a stored mask PNG of the same size. This happens on every select or cleanup of a small object in a phone photo: 403 MB RSS on flat colour in my measurement, and seconds of PNG encoding on real photos.
   - Fix: crop the original and the parts to the bbox plus a margin first, then upscale only the crop.

6. **Soft edges carry the background colour.**
   - Where: `web/lib/image/sticker.ts:82`.
   - What: the RGB of edge pixels comes from the original, so semi-transparent pixels carry the table or paper colour with straight alpha. On dark tables this shows as a dark rim on totes and tiles.
   - Fix: decontaminate the edge band (inpaint RGB from inside pixels, or blur-fill RGB under alpha before `joinChannel`).

7. **Every enclosed hole is filled, whatever its size.**
   - Where: `web/lib/image/mask.ts:284-286`.
   - What: `fillHoles` runs per part and again after close, so real openings are filled with background: a mug handle, the gap between a toy's arm and its body, the inside of a large "O". This trades the reported white holes for table-coloured patches.
   - Fix: fill only holes under a size threshold, for example under 2 % of the part's area.

8. **Approve can flip an archived product back to published.**
   - Where: `web/lib/pipeline/products.ts:178`.
   - What: `approveListing` does not check `product.status`, so a creator can set an archived product back to `published`. It stays invisible only because the shop also filters on deleted artworks.
   - Fix: reject approve and run on archived products (409).

9. **The new tests do not exercise the real rebuild or the cleanup route.**
   - Where: `web/test/artwork-adjust.test.ts:1377`, `web/test/artworks-route.test.ts`.
   - What: `rebuildProductFiles` is mocked and has no direct test. There is no test of the cleanup route itself: zod 400s, the route-level `invalid_candidate`, and that `phrase` is now ignored or rejected end to end. The "cross-creator 404" test is the mock returning null for an unknown id, not a different creator. `artwork-queries.test.ts` asserts SQL strings, which is brittle, but it does check the filters.
   - Fix: add a route test for `POST /artworks/{id}/cleanup` with a mocked pipeline, and one `rebuildProductFiles` test with fixture storage that asserts the product urls change and the status stays.

10. **`GET /artworks/{id}` can download the whole original for old rows.**
    - Where: `web/lib/pipeline/artworks.ts:185`.
    - What: for rows without `shapingTrace.imageSize`, `originalSize` reads the full original from Blob on every GET.
    - Fix: read the header only, or backfill `imageSize` on the first read.

11. **The stored mask is not at the original's size.**
    - Where: `web/lib/pipeline/artworks.ts:57-58`.
    - What: the artwork's `maskUrl` is stored at the upscaled size (`tw×th`). The docs do not say so, and any later consumer that assumes original size would be off by the factor `k`.
    - Fix: store the mask at original size, or document it.

### New issues introduced
- **Important: the container rule can drop a close-up object and auto-select one of its sub-parts.**
  - Where: `web/lib/ai/meta/sam.ts:73-75`, with `web/lib/pipeline/artworks.ts:199`.
  - What: any result over 40 % of the frame whose box contains another result's box is dropped, whatever its score.
  - Why: a creator who follows the 422 advice ("filling most of the frame") often produces a toy or wallet over 40 % of the frame. SAM commonly also returns a part of it, such as a scarf or a printed face from "drawing", with IoU < 0.8. Then the whole object is dropped. The part becomes the only candidate, it is small, and it is auto-selected, so the creator silently gets a cut of the scarf. The `bigToy` test covers only "contains nothing". No live photo exercised a close-up object (the bear and wallet photos fell below 40 % or had no inner result).
  - Fix: treat a result as a container only when its score is below the best result it contains (the live data separates them: paper 0.41–0.56 vs drawings 0.62–0.94), or only when it comes from the object phrase and its contents come from "drawing". Also skip auto-select whenever a container was dropped. Add a test with a 60 % object at 0.9 and an inner part at 0.7, expecting the object to be kept.
- **Minor: listing check and product files can disagree after a partial failure.**
  - Where: `web/lib/pipeline/products.ts` (`rebuildProductFiles`).
  - What: `setListingPrintCheck` runs before `updateProduct`. If the product update fails, the listing check already describes files the product does not point at.
  - Fix: write the check after `updateProduct`, or in the same batch.
- **Minor: negative points label the original at full size.**
  - Where: `web/lib/image/mask.ts` (`removePiecesAt`).
  - What: it runs `components()` on each original-size candidate mask (two Int32 arrays of 48 MB each per candidate on a 12 MP photo) to look up a few labels.
  - Fix: flood-fill from the tapped pixels instead.
